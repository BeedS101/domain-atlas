The current owner signs this exact transfer in the browser. Export and import (portable JSON)

The exported file contains the asset id, issuer identities and keys, the complete roster, the issuance signatures, the signed issuance and transfer policies, discovery references and capabilities, the ownership authority and the signed ownership history. It is verified here with Web Crypto. A file on its own can prove who issued the asset and how it has changed hands, but not what is current: only the registry can say that.

What this proves, and what it does not. Each issuing domain signs only after validating the asset itself under its own pinned configuration, and the registry executes only a transfer the current owner signed and a satisfied approval certificate. This is not atomic settlement across independent ledgers and not Byzantine fault tolerant consensus: one designated state authority holds ownership state and could withhold or roll back history, which a fresh signed state attestation and the signed history make detectable but not impossible.

  
Forged signatures, tampered metadata, duplicate approvals, replays, stale state, competing transfers, unavailable peers, restarts and service relocation are exercised by test/manual-multi-issuer-php.js, not by controls here. Public sessions are limited, rate-limited and expire.
